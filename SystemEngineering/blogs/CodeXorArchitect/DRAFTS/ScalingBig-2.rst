Scaling Big (2)
===============

.. post:: 27/Oct 2026
   :tags: CodeXorArchitect
   :category: LinkedIn-Post

   :reading-time:	ToDo                    
   :LinkedIn: 		ToDo                    

   Suppose you want to read a really nice novel --- but which one? Even a modest library or bookshop has more books than
   you can ever read. Maybe you ask yourself: *How to find that great one, fast?  Probably not; everybody follows the
   same approach. That ‘algorithm’ ---described below--- itself isn't that relevant!

   What is relevant is, is the notion that it exist! And that you can use it to become a better designer.
   |BR|
   And that *it’s kind of mandatory for a genuine architect!*

Architects should make big systems feel simple. When a team is added to speed up development, they should quickly get
insight into the system --- and so, read some great documentation. But not all, and certainly they shouldn't need to read
all code!
|BR|
See here an analogy between a bookshop with novels and your library full of code, designs, user manuals, meeting minutes
and more.

When you write, you should write for an audience, and make sure your readers will find that document! Therefore, you
don’t need to be a novelist; you need to be a craftsman and grasp “the algorithm”.

That procedure isn't about the *best “book”*, nor about searching or finding it. People start by excluding. When you
want a defective, you skip the science department, you don't enter the 18+ floor, and ignore the kids’ corner, etc. At a
glance, more than 70% is ignored. When you document isn't there, you have wasted your time.
|BR|
Sometimes a book is completely ignored because the bookseller didn't understand it and shelved it wrongly.

Eventually, when you find the last few shelves, you turn your head 90% and scan the spines. Again you fly over most titles;
too thin, too thick, or the wrong color of pink. Utterly, you slide a few books forward, pick some of them actually in
your hand, read the back --- and put them back. Of all those thousands of books, you open maybe a few dozen. Typically to
read only the 1st paragraph...

What should you learn from that? Make sure it’s found; that a library shelver can put it in the best cabinet. The title
should be pin-on. And spend time on the ‘lead’.

Long story short: Become an architect! Don’t write for people who already know; explain big, complicated systems in a
simple, understandable way! It doesn't matter whether you write a novel, a design document or even code. Make it simple!
If you can’t make it simple, it’s maybe too complicated for you.

An architect isn't a bricklayer, so why should a software architect write code? Don’t they have more important
work?
|BR|
This is a hard question and depends heavily on subjective personal preferences. But not answering does degrade the
craft. Whereas *genuine embedded systems/software architects* are needed more than ever.

Let us start with the goal of a software team. That is to produce code --- the asset that brings value. Other activities
are **‘overhead’**, unless they increase the yield.  This gives us a simple guideline for the worth of non-coding
tasks. Every hour of labor the architect spends should (all in all) deliver more than an hour of coding. Actually, more
like 2 hours, as they are expensive. This already hints that "coding" isn't a major objective.
|BR|
In software, however, the difference isn’t binary. The path from programmer to architect is like a sliding spanner; one
can be a bit of both. It doesn't help to define the role, yet enables to grow and mature architects - from level *L1*'
upwards (More on that in :ref:`3Amigos_P4`).

The product’s size & complexity, as well as the team’s maturity, dictates the architects *'Level'* that is needed.
|BR|
A small product with very experienced developers, doesn't demand a full-time architect; a L1 will do. With many young
coworker you need more maturity -- maybe a 'L3'. For a big, multidisciplinary, products with many products, the slider
should be even farther.
|BR|
It doesn't mater which level you are now, there is always room to improve, to raise the bar.

Plz, Next time you start coding — if being an architect is truly your ambition — just stop! Think for a minute. Maybe
someone else can do that too, maybe even cheaper.  |BR| When you spend 1 hour coaching 2 juniors, to make them 3% more
efficient, you more than double your value!  The team produces this sprint exactly the same -- and next sprint even
more.

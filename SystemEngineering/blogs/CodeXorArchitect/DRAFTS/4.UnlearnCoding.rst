An architect isn't a bricklayer, so why should a software architect write code? Doesn't (s)he have more important
work?
|BR|
This is a hard question and depends heavily on subjective personal preferences. But not answering does degrade the
craft. Whereas *“genuine embedded system/software architects”* are needed more than ever.

Let us start with the goal of a software team. That is to produce code --- the asset that brings value. Other activities
are **“overhead”**, unless they increase the yield.
|BR|
This gives us a simple guideline for the worth of non-coding tasks. Every hour
of labor the architect spends should (all in all) deliver more than an hour of coding. Actually more like 2 hours,
as they are expensive. This already hints that "coding" isn't a major objective.

In software, however, it isn’t binary. The path from programmer to architect is more like a slider. This can be
confusing, but it also helps us grow and mature. (More on that in :ref:`3Amigos_P4`).
|BR|
Mostly, the product size & complexity, and the team’s maturity dictate where that slider should be. For a small
product with very experienced developers, you don't need a full-time architect. But for a big, multi-team project —
especially with many young coworkers — the slider should probably be at the far end.

Next time you start coding — if being an architect is truly your ambition — just stop! Think for a minute. Maybe
somebody else can do that too, maybe even cheaper.
|BR|
When you spend 1 hour coaching 1 or 2 juniors, to make them 1.25% more efficient, you more than double your value!
The team produces this sprint exactly the same -- and next sprint even more.

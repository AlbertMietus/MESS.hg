Software BOM
============

.. post:: 3/Nov/2026
   :tags: CodeXorArchitect
   :category: LinkedIn-Post

   :reading-time:	ToDo
   :LinkedIn: 		ToDo

   When developing an embedded system, most engineers ask, *“What does it do?”* But *“How many are we going to
   sell?”* is just as interesting --- as it defines the architecture!
   |BR|
   Embedded systems are built from software and hardware. Traditionally, a lower ‘BOM’ --- the cost of the electronics,
   mechanics, etc. --- has always been the goal, as software has only development costs.

   But is that always true?
   |BR|
   For huge sales volumes (millions/week), it is. But for low numbers, the architect should correct that illusion!

Price calculation is a profession --- but the basics are simple. We can ignore expenses unrelated to
development ---like marketing & promotion.  Hardware costs, especially for “computer electronics”, do matter, however.
|BR|
Here, the architect has a role.

In a nutshell: Divide the (software) development investment by the total number of units to get the cost per unit.
Roughly, you can add it to the BOM and compare it with alternatives. Adding computing power raises the BOM, which is
fine when it lowers the software cost per unit even more. This isn't simple arithmetic, as most numbers are based on
predictions and forecasts. But a price expert values a solid estimate over details, too.
|BR|
The hardest part is fighting tradition, as software costs are ignored for years.

Let me give you two examples to inspire you.
|BR|
One company almost went out of business when they forgot about the RTOS licensing price. It should be on the BOM,
but as it was software...
|BR|
The second is Embedded Linux. That success comes from the same formula. Compared to classic embedded systems, it makes
writing code easier and faster. Or rephrased: the software development investment dropped significantly.  Even at midsize
volumes, it is currently competitive. The extra hardware (BOM) costs are a fraction of the reduced development!

Although architecture often provides technical solutions, it should answer business questions. Finding the balance
between saving costs and optimizing investment is one of them.
|BR|
Do yourself a favour and practice this algorithm. Try it with your current products; with hindsight, it's a bit
easier. And you learn faster.

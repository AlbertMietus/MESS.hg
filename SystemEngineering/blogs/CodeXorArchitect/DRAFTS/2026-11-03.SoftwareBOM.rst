Software BOM
============

.. post:: 3/Nov/2026
   :tags: CodeXorArchitect
   :category: LinkedIn-Post

   :reading-time:	ToDo
   :LinkedIn: 		ToDo

   When developing an embedded system, most engineers ask, *“What does it do?”* However, the question *“How many are we going to
   sell?”* is at least as interesting --- as it defines the architecture!
   |BR|
   Embedded systems are built from software and hardware. Traditionally, a lower ‘BOM’ --- the cost of the electronics,
   mechanics, etc. --- has always been the goal, as software has only development costs.

   But is that always true?
   |BR|
   For huge sales volumes (millions/week), it is. But for low numbers, the architect should correct that illusion!

Calculating the sales price is a profession in its own, but the basics are simple. We can ignore expenses unrelated to
development ---like marketing & promotion.  Hardware costs, especially for “computer electronics”, do matter, however.
|BR|
Here, the architect has a role.

In a nutshell: Dividing the (software) development investment by the total number of units sold gives the cost per
unit.  Roughly, you can add it to the BOM and play with it. Adding computing power raises the BOM, which is fine when it
lowers the software investment even more. This isn't simple arithmetic, as most numbers are based on predictions. But
price experts are always working with forecasts.
|BR|
The hardest part is fighting tradition, as software costs are ignored for years.

Let me give you two examples to inspire you.
|BR|
One company almost went out of business. They forgot to add the RTOS licensing price because it was software...
|BR|
The second is Embedded Linux. That success comes from the same formula. Compared to classic embedded systems, it makes
writing code easier and faster. Or rephrased: the software development investment dropped significantly.  Even at midsize
volumes, it is currently competitive. The extra hardware (BOM) costs are a fraction of the reduced development!

Although architecture often provides technical solutions, it should answer business questions --- like finding the
balance between saving costs and optimising investment.
|BR|
Do yourself a favour and practice this algorithm ---even afterwards. Try it with your current products; with hindsight,
it's a bit easier.

Software BOM
============

.. post:: 3/Nov/2026
   :tags: CodeXorArchitect
   :category: LinkedIn-Post

   :reading-time:	ToDo
   :LinkedIn: 		ToDo

   When developing an embedded system, most engineers ask, *“What does it do?”* However, the question *“How many are we going to
   sell?”* is at least as interesting --- as it defines the architecture!
   |BR|
   Embedded systems are built from software and hardware. Traditionally, a lower ‘BOM’ --- the cost of the electronics,
   mechanics, etc. --- has always been the goal, as software has only development costs.

   But is that always true?
   |BR|
   For huge sales volumes (millions/week), it is. But for low numbers, the architect should correct that illusion!

Calculating the (sales) prices is a profession on its own, but the basics are simple. There are many costs, like
marketing & promotion, that we can ignore --- as unrelated to development costs.  The costs of the electronics do
matter, however.
|BR|
Here, the architect has a role.


In a nutshell, the (software) development investment divided by the total sales volume contributes to the unit price.
|BR|
Roughly, you can add it to the BOM and play with it. More computing power raises the BOM, which is fine when it lowers
the software investment more. This isn't simple arithmetic, as most numbers are based on predictions. But price
experts are always working with forecasts.
|BR|
The hardest part is fighting tradition, as software costs are ignored for years.

Let me give you two examples to inspire you.
|BR|
One company almost went out of business, as they forgot to add the RT-OS licensing price to the BOM, as it was
software... The second is Embedded Linux. That success is based on the same formula. Compared to classic embedded
systems, it's much easier and faster to write code for.
|BR|
Or rephrased: the software development investment went down hard. Even for a midsize volume, it is currently
competitive; the extra costs for hardware are a fraction of the savings in development costs!

Although architecture often provides technical solutions, it should answer business questions --- like finding the balance
between cost and investment.
|BR|
Do yourself a favour and practice this algorithm ---even afterwards. Try it with your current products; with hindsight
it's a bit easier.

Act as an SW-Ingenieur!
=======================

.. post:: 2026/Nov/18
   :tags: CodeXorArchitect
   :category: LinkedIn-Post

   :reading-time:	1min38
   :LinkedIn: 		ToDo

   Dutch law protects the title *“ingenieur”*. Unlike the English term *engineer*, you can’t use it without completing a
   government-accredited, formal technical education. A **SW-Ingenieur** is university-educated and trained to solve
   technical challenges ---including designing software architecture.

   Speaking about (embedded) software *architecture*: The terms ‘architect’ and ‘architecture’ are even more confusing!
   |BR|
   It’s time to make it clear --- as an architect, I feel obligated.


Again, Dutch law has a saying about this. You are only an ‘architect’ when you're registered in the official Dutch
Architects Register, which accepts people with experience in buildings only. Most ‘architects’ are also ‘ingenieur’, but
not all ---some are trained in the Art Academy department.
|BR|
Luckily,  the term architect with a prefix, like “software-architect”, is allowed. But all formal guarantees are then
gone.

Still, it gives us some hints. A ‘SW-Ingenieur’ is well-trained and should be able to deliver more than just code.
|BR|
That is why many Dutch HighTech companies ask for *Software-Ingenieurs* (‘ir/ing’ or ‘MSc/BSc’), not just Engineers. It
is not about the software --- tools can generate that nowadays. An (SW-)Ingenieur can solve challenges with technical
solutions.
|BR|
That is one reason why CodeAI isn’t a big threat for SW-Ingenieurs. A genuine ingenieur uses technology to solve real
problems.

Typically, an ingenieur’s engineering is about finding the best route, balancing cost & performance, resulting in a
reliable, maintainable appliance. An SW-Ingenieur should never rush to a silver bullet!
|BR|
High-Tech, Embedded “Sovereign” Software isn't about the code. It’s about what it accomplishes: a full-swing system.

But when an SW-Ingenieur is trained to design such a system, what should an (embedded system/software) architect do
extra?
|BR|
It’s about the “extra”. Any genuine architect should also be an ‘SW-Ingenieur’. But that doesn't imply coding ---soon we
will have tools for that. But it does imply that the (system/software) architect should act as an (SW) Ingenieur: Solve
real disputed points, typically with a technical approach.

I hope this helps. Still, we have one extra complication: when an SW-Ingenieur can “design the architecture” ... What is
the difference between ‘design’ and ‘architecture’? That, however, is stuff for a whole other :ref:`story
<layers-inversion>`!

For now, please, always act as an ingenieur! And help to plug the term ‘SW-Ingenieur’; that solves a big problem. Maybe
engineering software/code can be automated, but SW-Ingenieurs deliver value.

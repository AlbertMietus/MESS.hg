Act as a SW-Ingenieur!
======================

.. post:: 2026/Nov/18
   :tags: CodeXorArchitect
   :category: LinkedIn-Post

   :reading-time:	ToDo
   :LinkedIn: 		ToDo

   Dutch law protects the title *“ingenieur”*. Unlike the English term *engineer*, you are only allowed to use it after
   completing a government-accredited, formal technical education.  This implies that a **SW-Ingenieur** is
   university-educated and trained to solve technical challenges ---including designing the architecture.

   Speaking about *embedded software architecture*: The terms ‘architect’ and ‘architecture’ are even more confusing!
   |BR|
   It’s time to make it clear --- as an architect, I feel obligated.

Again, Dutch law has a say in this. An ‘architect’ must be registered in the official Dutch Architects Register, which
only accepts people with experience in buildings. Most ‘architects’ are also ‘ingenieur’, but not all -- some
are trained in the Art Academy department.
|BR|
Luckily, it is allowed to use the term architect with a prefix, like “software-architect”. However, that has no formal
guarantee whatsoever.

Still, it gives us some hints. A ‘SW-ingenieur’ is well-trained and should be able to deliver more than just code. That is
why many Dutch HighTech companies ask for *software ingenieurs* (‘ir/ing’ or ‘MSc/BSc’), not just Engineers. It is not
about the software --- tools can generate that nowadays. An (SW-)ingenieur can solve challenges with technical solutions.
|BR|
That is one reason why CodeAI isn't a big threat for SW-Ingenieurs. A genuine ingenieur can use technology, but solves
real problems.

Typically, an ingenieur’s engineering is about finding the best route; balancing cost & performance, resulting in a
reliable, maintainable appliance. A SW-Ingenieur should never rush to a silver bullet!
|BR|
HighTech, Embedded “Sovereign” Software isn't about the code. It’s about what it accomplishes: a full-swing system.

But when an SW-Ingenieur is trained to design such a system, what should an (embedded system/software) architect do
extra?
|BR|
It’s about the “extra”. Any genuine architect should also be an ‘SW-Ingenieur’. But that doesn't imply coding ---soon we
have tools for that. But it does imply the (system/software) architect should act as an (SW) ingenieur: Solve real
disputed points, typically with a technical approach.

I hope this helps. Still, we have one extra complication: when a SW-Ingenieur can “design the architecture” ... What is
the difference between ‘design’ and ‘architecture’? That, however, is stuff for a whole other :ref:`story
<layers-inversion>`!

For now, please, always act as an Ingenieur! And help to plug the term ‘SW-Ingenieur’; that solves a big problem! For
all of us.
